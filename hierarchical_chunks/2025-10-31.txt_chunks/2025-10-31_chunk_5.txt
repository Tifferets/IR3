HONORING JUDY AHMANN Mr. THOMPSON of California. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Judy Ahmann, who has been named the 2025 Member of the Year by the Napa County Farm Bureau. Judy is being recognized for her decades of advocacy for agriculture and her enduring commitment to the Farm Bureau and our Napa County's agricultural community. Ms. Ahmann was born in Omaha, Nebraska, and later moved with her family to Long Beach, California. She attended Long Beach State College, graduating in 1966, a year after marrying her husband, John. Judy began her professional career at IBM, but her passion for the land soon led her and John to purchase their first 12 acres in California. After a brief relocation to New Jersey for work, they returned to California in 1970 and settled in our Napa County, where they purchased 300 acres and began raising cattle. In 1986, they acquired more than 3,000 acres along Lake Berryessa, establishing Running Deer Ranch. With no prior ranching experience, the Ahmanns turned to the Napa County Farm Bureau and local Cattlewomen's and Cattlemen's Associations for guidance so they could raise their three daughters, Christina, Anna, and Erica, in the ranching tradition. Ms. Ahmann's commitment to our agricultural community is clear from her extensive advocacy work. She became a long-standing member and leader in California CattleWomen, serving as president at both the local and state levels, including as state president in 2007 and 2008. She later joined the Executive Board of American National CattleWomen, where her leadership earned her recognition as National CattleWoman of the Year in 2014. In 2010, Judy was selected as a member of Common Threads California, which honors women for their volunteer leadership and contributions to agriculture. Judy has rightfully been recognized for her advocacy at the national level. In 2013, she testified before the House Natural Resources Subcommittee on Public Lands and Environmental Regulation in support of the Berryessa Snow Mountain National Conservation Area Act. Her testimony focused on the need to balance conservation efforts with the preservation of working ranchlands and grazing operations for future generations. Ms. Ahmann's dedication to agriculture extends to supporting the next generation of farmers and ranchers. She and her family have been generous supporters of youth in agriculture, donating time and raising funds for scholarships benefiting local 4-H and Future Farmers of America students. She has been a steadfast supporter of the Napa County Farm Bureau, actively promoting agricultural policy, education, and community engagement. In 2010, Judy and John placed a conservation easement on 1,275 acres of Running Deer Ranch through the California Rangeland Trust, permanently protecting the ranch as a working landscape while preserving its vital wildlife habitat. They also granted a trail easement to open public access to 9,100 acres of Bureau of Land Management land that had been landlocked for more than 150 years.
