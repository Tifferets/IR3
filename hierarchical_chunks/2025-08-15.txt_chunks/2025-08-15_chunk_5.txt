Born in San Francisco in 1941 to George and Lucille Sattui, Mr. Sattui grew up understanding the importance of family legacy and hard work. He earned his bachelor of science in accounting and finance from San Jose State University in 1965, followed by his master of business administration with an emphasis in Marketing from the University of California, Berkeley in 1969. The story of V. Sattui Winery began in 1885 when Mr. Sattui's great- grandfather, Vittorio Sattui, immigrated from Genoa, Italy, and established a thriving San Francisco winery that operated for 35 years before Prohibition forced its closure. In 1975, nearly four decades later, Mr. Sattui revived the family dream by re-establishing V. Sattui Winery in our Napa Valley. When the winery opened its doors on March 4, 1976, it became the 46th winery to operate in Napa Valley since Prohibition, with Mr. Sattui's vision of creating a Napa d'Italia where guests could experience the good life through exceptional wine and warm hospitality. V. Sattui's commitment to excellence has been widely recognized. The winery has been named ``Winery of the Year'' at the California State Fair four times, earned the title ``Best Winery in the Western U.S.'' at the Critics Challenge Wine Competition, and received ``Domestic Winery of the Year'' from the San Diego International Wine & Spirits Challenge. In 2023, V. Sattui converted to 100 percent organic farming across 200 acres spanning four counties, demonstrating Mr. Sattui's dedication to sustainable agriculture and environmental protection. Mr. Sattui's commitment to our community extends far beyond winemaking. He has donated $1.6 million to support the Calistoga Boys & Girls Club and contributed $1 million to the Calistoga School District to expand its preschool program. His generosity has also benefited St. Helena Public Schools Foundation, Napa Valley education programs, and disaster relief efforts, including a $100,000 contribution to support victims of the 2017 fires. Through conservation easements with the Land Trust of Napa Valley, Mr Sattui has also protected more than 600 acres from future development, ensuring these lands remain available for agriculture and open space in perpetuity. Mr. Sattui's dedication to health and medical research is reflected in his $4.5 million donation to the Gladstone Institute for heart health research. He has also supported innovative environmental initiatives, including his investment in Resynergi of Santa Rosa to convert plastic waste into energy, and his support for Meals on Wheels
